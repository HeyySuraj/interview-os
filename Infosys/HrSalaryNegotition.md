Yes. Since you have **3+ YOE, current CTC around ₹5 LPA, and you're interviewing for Senior Software Engineer (Node.js + React)**, you should go into the HR/compensation discussion with a clear strategy.

One important point first: **₹12 LPA is an aggressive ask from a ₹5 LPA current CTC, but it is not something you need to withdraw just because the percentage hike looks large.** Current public salary submissions for Infosys show a wide range even for senior software roles—for example, recent Glassdoor submissions include ₹14 LPA for a 4–6 YOE Senior Software Engineer and ₹13 LPA for a 4–6 YOE Senior Software Developer in Pune. These are individual reported salaries, not an official Infosys pay band, so they should be treated as market signals rather than guarantees. ([Glassdoor][1])

There are also recent candidate discussions specifically around **3+ YOE / ₹5 LPA current CTC**, where candidates discuss negotiating toward ₹9–10 LPA, while another 4-YOE candidate reported being offered ₹7.5 LPA initially and later receiving a ₹14 LPA offer elsewhere. These are anecdotes, not reliable benchmarks, but they show why current CTC alone shouldn't be the only thing you use to frame your expectation. ([Reddit][2])

## Your numbers

I'd keep these numbers in your head:

| Number                               | Your position  |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------- |
| Current CTC                          | **₹5 LPA**     |
| Expected/asked                       | **₹12 LPA**    |
| Current-company expected revision    | **~₹6 LPA**    |
| Ideal Infosys outcome                | **₹10–12 LPA** |
| Negotiation zone                     | **₹9–12 LPA**  |
| Don't volunteer a lower number early | **Yes**        |

The most important thing is:

**Don't negotiate based on "I currently get ₹5 LPA, so give me X% hike."**

Negotiate based on:

**Role + experience + skills + responsibilities + interview performance + market compensation.**

---

# 1. If HR asks: "What is your current CTC?"

Tell the truth:

> "My current CTC is ₹5 LPA."

Don't unnecessarily add:

> "But my company is going to increase it to ₹6 LPA."

Unless they specifically ask about an upcoming appraisal/revision.

If they ask:

**"Do you have any upcoming appraisal?"**

Then say:

> "Yes, my current compensation is around ₹5 LPA, and based on the expected revision it may move to around ₹6 LPA. However, for this opportunity I'm evaluating the compensation based on the role, responsibilities and my overall experience."

That's much better than hiding it or pretending ₹6 LPA is already your CTC.

---

# 2. If HR asks: "What are your expectations?"

You already told them **₹12 LPA**.

Don't suddenly change it to ₹8 LPA because you're worried.

Say:

> **"Considering my 3+ years of experience, my hands-on experience with Node.js, React, TypeScript, backend APIs, MongoDB, Redis, AWS and production systems, and the responsibilities of this Senior Software Engineer role, I'm looking at around ₹12 LPA."**

Then add:

> **"However, I'm open to discussing the overall compensation structure based on the role and responsibilities."**

That's your default answer.

---

# 3. If HR says: "That's a very high hike from your current CTC."

This is probably the **most important response to practice**.

Don't become defensive.

Say:

> "I understand that the percentage hike looks high compared to my current CTC. However, my expectation is based more on the role and responsibilities rather than just the percentage hike from my current salary.
>
> I have 3+ years of practical experience working with Node.js, React and backend systems, including APIs, Redis, MongoDB, AWS, queues and third-party integrations.
>
> Since this is a Senior Software Engineer role, I'm looking for compensation that is aligned with the responsibilities of the position."

**This is the answer I'd memorize.**

---

# 4. If they say: "We can only offer ₹7 LPA."

Don't immediately say yes/no.

Say:

> "I appreciate the offer. However, ₹7 LPA would be difficult for me considering the role and my experience. Is there any flexibility to revise the package closer to my expectation?"

Then **stop talking**.

Let HR respond.

Don't immediately negotiate against yourself:

> "Okay, maybe 8?"

Wait.

---

# 5. If they say ₹8 LPA

Say:

> "Thank you for checking. I was expecting something closer to ₹10–12 LPA considering the role and my experience. Is there any possibility of moving closer to ₹10 LPA?"

Now you've moved from:

**12 → 10**

without yourself volunteering 8.

---

# 6. If they say ₹9 LPA

I'd respond:

> "₹9 LPA is closer to what I was expecting. Considering the senior role and my 3+ years of experience, could you please check if there is any possibility of bringing it closer to ₹10 LPA?"

That's a reasonable counter.

---

# 7. If they say ₹10 LPA

Now you need to decide your personal minimum.

You could say:

> "₹10 LPA is much closer to my expectation. If you can make the overall package around ₹10–11 LPA, I would be very comfortable proceeding."

But **don't promise you'll join** unless you're actually prepared to do so.

---

# 8. If they say: "₹10 LPA is our final offer."

You can ask one final question:

> "Understood. Is there any flexibility in the fixed component or joining bonus, if the overall CTC cannot be increased?"

This is important because **CTC isn't the whole story**.

Ask about:

* Fixed salary
* Variable pay
* Joining bonus
* Retention bonus
* Performance bonus
* Notice-period conditions
* Location
* Any bond/service agreement
* Salary revision cycle

A ₹10 LPA package with ₹9.5 LPA fixed can be materially different from ₹10 LPA with a large variable component.

---

# 9. If they say "We calculate everything based on current CTC."

Don't argue.

Say:

> "I understand that current CTC is one of the factors in the compensation process. At the same time, I'd request you to consider the experience and skill requirements of the role as well. I'm confident I can contribute at the Senior Software Engineer level, so I'd appreciate it if you could check whether there is flexibility in the band."

Very professional.

# 10. If HR says "Your current salary is only ₹5 LPA."

Your response:

I'm looking for the next role based on the skills and responsibilities required for the position rather than simply applying a percentage hike to my current CTC."

This is particularly important for you.

# 15. If they ask: "What is your minimum?"

This is where you need to be careful.

**Don't give your minimum early.**

Say:

> "I'd prefer to first understand the complete compensation structure and the role expectations. Based on what we've discussed, my expectation is around ₹12 LPA, and I'm open to discussing it."

If they insist:

> "I'd like to stay as close as possible to ₹10–12 LPA, depending on the fixed and variable components."

Only say a number you're genuinely willing to accept.

---

# 16. If they say "Give us a flexible number."

Say:

> "I'm targeting around ₹12 LPA, but I'm open to a reasonable discussion depending on the overall compensation structure."

That's better than:

> "10–12."

Because if you say **10–12**, HR hears **10**.

# 17. The biggest mistake: negotiating against yourself

Imagine this conversation:

**HR:** Expected?

**You:** 12.

**HR:** That's high.

**You:** Okay, 10.

**HR:** Still high.

**You:** 9.

**HR:** We can give 8.

You've done HR's job for them. 😄

Instead:

**HR:** Expected?

**You:** ₹12 LPA.

**HR:** That's high.

**You:** I understand. My expectation is based on the role and my 3+ years of experience. Is there flexibility in the compensation band?

**HR:** We can give 8.

**You:** I appreciate that. Could you please check if there is flexibility to move closer to ₹10 LPA?

**HR:** Maybe 9.

**You:** If you can get closer to ₹10, I'd be comfortable discussing the offer further.

That's negotiation.
